Trapping Rain Water

Track the Limiting Wall ⏱ O(n) time 💾 O(1) space

Problem

Given n non-negative integers representing an elevation map, compute how much water it can trap after raining.

Input:  height = [0,1,0,2,1,0,1,3,2,1,2,1]
Output: 6

Intuition

The water trapped above any bar is min(tallest wall to its left, tallest wall to its right) − its own height. The brute force recomputes both max walls for every bar — O(n²). Two pointers get it in one pass.

Track leftMax and rightMax as you close in from both ends. Whichever side has the smaller current wall is the side whose water level is already fully determined — its own max is guaranteed to be the limiting (smaller) wall, since the other side's max is at least as tall as the pointer that hasn't moved yet. Process that side, add trapped water, advance inward.

Full Explanation

Water sits above a bar up to the height of the shorter of the tallest wall to its left and the tallest wall to its right. Computing both directions for every bar separately takes quadratic time, but two pointers converging from the outside can determine the answer for one side at a time, in a single pass.

We track the running maximum height seen so far from the left and from the right. At each step we compare those two running maximums: whichever is smaller tells us which pointer to advance, because that side's water level is already fully decided — no bar further inside on the taller side could ever become the limiting wall for it. We move that pointer, update its running max, and add the gap between the running max and the current bar's height as trapped water. The pointers meet once every bar has been resolved.
