Container With Most Water

Shrink the Shorter Wall ⏱ O(n) time 💾 O(1) space

Problem

Given heights height[i] for n vertical lines, find two lines that, together with the x-axis, form a container holding the most water. Return that maximum area.

Input:  height = [1,8,6,2,5,4,8,3,7]
Output: 49

Intuition

Area is width × min(left height, right height) — a container is only as tall as its shorter wall. Starting with the widest possible container (both ends), any move that narrows it needs to at least try for a taller wall to have a chance of beating the current area.

Move the pointer at the shorter wall inward. Moving the taller one can only hurt — width shrinks and the limiting height stays the same or gets worse. Moving the shorter one is the only move that could reveal a taller wall and offset the lost width. Track the best area seen as the pointers converge.

Full Explanation

The amount of water a pair of walls can hold is the distance between them multiplied by the shorter of the two heights — water always spills over the lower wall. We start with the two outermost lines, which gives the maximum possible width, and record that area as our starting best.

The key insight is which pointer to move next. If we move the taller wall inward, the width shrinks and the limiting height is still at best the same short wall, so the area cannot improve — that move is provably useless. Moving the shorter wall inward is the only move that has any chance of finding a taller wall to compensate for the lost width. So we always advance the pointer at the shorter side, recompute the area, and keep the best value seen, until the pointers meet.
